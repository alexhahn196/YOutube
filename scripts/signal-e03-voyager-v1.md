[SEGMENT 3: THE EDGE — what it actually found]

[SCORE IN — tense, vast]

Voyager's plasma density instrument had died back in 1980 — so the team had to wait for the Sun itself to erupt, slap the surrounding plasma, and make it ring like a struck bell before the crossing could be confirmed.

[ON SCREEN: Gurnett et al., Science 2013 — „plasma oscillations · crossing 25 Aug 2012 · 121.6 AU"]

Claim one: *"Voyager 1 turned around."*

Voyager 1 is coasting at seventeen kilometers per second — sixty-one thousand kilometers an hour, faster than the Sun's gravity can ever call back. Its thrusters are attitude jets: they fire in bursts of a few *milliseconds*, to rotate the craft, not to steer it. It can no more turn around than a rifle bullet can. And its cameras? Powered off on February 14th, 1990 — thirty-four minutes after the Pale Blue Dot. They have never opened again.

Claim two: *"4 MINUTES AGO: Voyager's shocking discovery—"*

Light itself takes almost twenty-four hours to arrive from Voyager 1. Nothing — *nothing* — about this spacecraft can be four minutes old.
