### ▐ SEGMENT 2 — THE EVIDENCE  (4:00–12:30)
*[VISUAL: On-Screen-Label „THE EVIDENCE". Wechsel zu nüchternerem, daten-getriebenem Look — echtes NASA/Hubble/JWST-Material einmischen.]*

> Okay. Let's actually look at what our best telescopes measured. Because the real object is stranger than the clickbait — just not in the way you'd think.
>
> **Fact one: where it came from.** Astronomers traced its motion and its chemistry, and the conclusion is staggering. 3I/ATLAS has been wandering the galaxy for an estimated **ten to twelve billion years** — meaning it is likely **older than the Sun, older than the Earth**, older than our entire solar system. We are looking at a genuine relic from another part of the Milky Way.
>
> **Fact two: how big it is.** Under all the drama, the solid nucleus is small — current estimates put it at **under about 1.3 kilometers**, less than a mile across. A city-sized chunk of ancient ice and rock.
>
> **Fact three: the trajectory.** It reached its closest point to the Sun — perihelion — on **October 29th, 2025**, passing at about 1.36 times the Earth–Sun distance, between the orbits of Earth and Mars. At its fastest it moved at roughly **68 kilometers per second** relative to the Sun. It never came anywhere near Earth. It then swung past Jupiter in **March 2026** — and it is now heading back out into interstellar space, fading as it goes.
>
> *[VISUAL: Zeitleiste — Discovery Jul 2025 → Perihel Okt 2025 → Jupiter Mär 2026 → leaving.]*
>
> Now the interesting part — the anomalies. Because they're real. Let's take them one at a time.
>
> **The backwards tail.** Normal comets grow a tail that's blown **away** from the Sun by sunlight and solar wind. 3I/ATLAS developed what's called an **anti-tail** — pointing, at times, **toward** the Sun. The Hubble Space Telescope caught this geometry in July 2025, and the Keck telescope confirmed it. Spooky? A little. But astronomers have a natural explanation: it points to the object venting gas and dust **unevenly** from specific spots — not the smooth, sunlight-driven behavior of a typical comet, but still a physical, natural process.
>
> **The inside-out chemistry.** This is the big one. When the James Webb Space Telescope measured the gas cloud around it, the composition was almost the **opposite** of a normal comet: roughly **87% carbon dioxide, about 9% carbon monoxide, and only around 4% water.** Most comets are dominated by water ice. This one barely has any. That's genuinely unusual — but it may simply tell us that 3I/ATLAS formed in a **frozen, distant region around its home star**, in conditions nothing in our solar system experienced.
>
> **The metal.** Its gas plume shows **nickel — but almost no iron.** In normal comets the two always appear together. And the nickel seems tied to a process called nickel-carbonyl formation, which on Earth we mostly see in **industrial metal refining.** That is exactly the kind of detail that makes a headline scream "manufactured." But here's the honest part: chemists have shown this can also happen through **natural low-temperature chemistry** in space. Unusual — not unnatural.
>
> *[VISUAL: HUD-Meter „SIGNAL STRENGTH" wackelt bei jeder Anomalie kurz nach oben, fällt dann zurück.]*
>
> So we have a genuinely weird object. Which brings us to the question everyone actually clicked for: **did we check if it's transmitting?**
>
> Yes. We did. Radio astronomers pointed their dishes at 3I/ATLAS and listened for any artificial signal. The search flagged around **470,000** candidate signals. Sounds dramatic — until you look closer. Every single one was traced to **human-made radio interference** here on Earth. When they pointed the telescope **away** from the object, the same signals showed up. The number of confirmed signals coming **from** 3I/ATLAS was **zero.**
>
> And the man who started the hype? Avi Loeb himself has now walked it back. After all the data came in, he stated the object is **"most likely natural"** — in his words, only slightly more anomalous than ʻOumuamua. The broader scientific community is clearer still: there is currently **no evidence** that 3I/ATLAS is anything other than a natural — if very unusual — comet.
